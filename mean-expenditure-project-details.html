Mean Expenditure Distribution of Sri Lanka

Spatial and Statistical Analysis using GIS

Project Overview

This project analyses the spatial variation of mean household expenditure across Sri Lanka using district-level census data. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and exploratory statistical techniques were applied to identify regional expenditure patterns and socio-economic disparities.

Study Area

The study area includes all administrative districts of Sri Lanka, allowing comparative analysis of expenditure characteristics across urban, semi-urban, and rural regions.

Data Sources

  • District-wise Mean Household Expenditure – Department of Census & Statistics
  • Sri Lanka Administrative Boundary Shapefile

Methodology

  • Data cleaning, preprocessing, and attribute joining
  • Choropleth mapping using graduated colour classification
  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
  • Histogram, Scatter Plot, and Box Plot analysis

Mean Expenditure Choropleth Map

The choropleth map visualises district-level variations in mean household expenditure, revealing distinct spatial patterns and expenditure concentration zones across Sri Lanka.

Key Findings

  • Noticeable variation in mean household expenditure among districts
  • Higher expenditure levels are concentrated in selected urban and coastal districts
  • Expenditure distribution shows right skewness with identifiable high-value outliers

Tools & Skills

  • ArcGIS Desktop, MS Excel
  • Socio-economic and spatial data analysis
  • Choropleth mapping techniques
  • Exploratory statistical visualisation

Conclusion

This project demonstrates the effective integration of GIS and statistical analysis to examine spatial expenditure patterns, providing insights that support regional planning and evidence-based socio-economic decision-making.
