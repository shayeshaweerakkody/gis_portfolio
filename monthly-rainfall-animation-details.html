Monthly Average Rainfall Distribution

Animated GIS-based Rainfall Visualisation (January–December)

Project Overview

This project presents an animated GIS visualisation of monthly average rainfall distribution across Sri Lanka from January to December. The animation highlights spatial and temporal rainfall variability to support climate, hydrological, and seasonal pattern analysis.

Study Area

The study covers the entire island of Sri Lanka, located between 5°55′–9°51′ N latitude and 79°41′–81°53′ E longitude. The country experiences distinct seasonal rainfall patterns influenced by monsoonal systems and topography.

Data Sources

  • Monthly average rainfall data – Meteorological Department of Sri Lanka
  • National administrative boundary data – Survey Department of Sri Lanka

Methodology

  • Preparation of monthly rainfall raster layers (January–December)
  • Classification using a consistent rainfall legend (mm)
  • Standardised cartographic layout for all monthly maps
  • Temporal sequencing and animation creation using GIS

Monthly Average Rainfall Animation

The animation illustrates monthly rainfall variation across Sri Lanka, with higher rainfall intensities observed during monsoonal periods. A consistent colour scheme enables direct comparison between months.

Rainfall Classification (mm)

  • 0 – 25 mm
  • 26 – 75 mm
  • 76 – 150 mm
  • 151 – 300 mm
  • 301 – 667 mm

Key Observations

  • Distinct seasonal rainfall patterns are visible across months
  • South-western and central regions receive higher rainfall
  • Dry zone areas show comparatively lower rainfall during most months
  • Temporal changes clearly reflect monsoonal influence

Tools & Skills

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • Raster data classification and symbology
  • Cartographic layout design
  • GIS animation and visual storytelling

Conclusion

This project strengthened skills in spatio-temporal climate data visualisation and GIS-based animation techniques. The animated rainfall maps provide an effective tool for communicating seasonal climate variability and supporting hydrological and environmental analysis.
